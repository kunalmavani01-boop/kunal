# PROMPTER Launch Plan

## Launch goal

Get real, relevant attention from technical early adopters, founders, creators, and AI-heavy users who can both try the product and give useful feedback.

## Best launch order

### Phase 1: polish and readiness

Do this before broad posting:

- make the GitHub repo the clean public home
- keep the README focused on product value, not internal history
- include one strong screenshot
- be ready to explain who PROMPTER is for in one sentence
- be ready to explain the difference between quick, guided, and expert modes

### Phase 2: technical audience launch

Start here first:

1. Hacker News `Show HN`
2. Indie Hackers
3. X and LinkedIn
4. a small number of relevant subreddits with platform-specific wording

### Phase 3: broader launch

Do this after feedback and cleanup:

1. Product Hunt
2. more founder and creator communities
3. short demo content

## Recommended positioning

Primary positioning:

`PROMPTER is a local-first browser app that turns rough prompts into stronger final prompts and helps people start faster with curated prompt packs.`

Short backup positioning:

`A local-first prompt refinement tool for people who want better prompts without learning prompt engineering from scratch.`

## Best channels

### 1. Hacker News

Why it fits:

- technical audience
- open to GitHub-first launches
- strong place to test whether the idea itself resonates

Recommended title:

`Show HN: PROMPTER - a local-first browser app to refine rough prompts`

Alternative titles:

- `Show HN: PROMPTER - turn rough prompts into clearer final prompts`
- `Show HN: PROMPTER - local-first prompt refinement with starter packs`

Suggested post body:

```text
I built PROMPTER, a local-first browser app for people who have rough prompt ideas but want a clearer final prompt without doing a full prompt-engineering workflow by hand.

The MVP focuses on two things:
- refining rough prompts into stronger final prompts
- helping users start from curated prompt packs when they do not want to begin from a blank box

It is currently a local-first browser app and the GitHub repo includes the Windows launch path.

I would especially love feedback on:
- whether the refinement output is meaningfully better
- whether the pack flow is intuitive
- which user group this feels most useful for

Repo:
https://github.com/kunalmavani01-boop/kunal/tree/codex/browser-mvp-prep
```

### 5. Reddit

Important:

- post only in subreddits where the rules allow it
- tailor the wording to the subreddit
- ask for feedback, not vanity traffic
- participate normally outside your own launch posts

Potential fits to evaluate manually:

- `r/SideProject`
- `r/InternetIsBeautiful`
- `r/ArtificialInteligence`
- `r/ChatGPT`
- `r/EntrepreneurRideAlong`
- specific productivity, founder, or creator communities if rules allow

Reddit account hygiene matters. Reddit's own reddiquette says self-promotion should be within reason, and a common rule of thumb is roughly 1 in 10 submissions being your own content.

## Product Hunt

Only do this once these are ready:

- cleaner repo or dedicated product URL
- stronger visual assets
- clear maker comment
- short demo or GIF
- polished first-time visitor flow

Product Hunt is useful for distribution, but it converts better when the project looks like a product launch rather than a raw branch release.

## Launch checklist

- GitHub page looks clean on first load
- README has a screenshot
- one-sentence positioning is locked
- launch copy is ready for HN, Indie Hackers, X, and LinkedIn
- you know what feedback you are asking for
- you are ready to reply quickly to comments for the first 24 hours

## What to track after posting

- which platform sends the most relevant comments
- whether people understand the product in under 10 seconds
- whether they care more about prompt quality or token savings
- whether they ask for hosted demo, Mac support, or browser extension support
- whether a narrower audience message performs better than the broad "prompt tool" framing
